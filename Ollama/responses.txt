'How big was the Roman Empire?'

Temperature - 0.01

At its peak, the Roman Empire covered approximately 5.9 million square miles (15.3 million km²), making it one of the largest empires in history.

To put that into perspective:

* It stretched from Britain in the northwest to Egypt and Syria in the southeast.
* From Spain and Gaul (modern-day France) in the west to Mesopotamia (modern-day Iraq) and Armenia in the east.
* And from the Atlantic Ocean in the west to the Euphrates River in the east.

The empire's vast territories included modern-day countries such as:

* Western Europe: Spain, France, Italy, Portugal, and parts of Germany
* North Africa: Egypt, Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, and Morocco
* Middle East: Israel, Palestine, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, and parts of Turkey

This immense territory was governed by a complex system of provinces, each with its own governor and administrative structure.

The Roman Empire's territorial extent peaked under its maximum territorial area, with Rome dominating three continents

Land area: Approximately 5-6 million square miles (13-16 million square kilometers), which is roughly 20% of the Earth's land surface.

This vast territory encompassed a significant portion of Europe, including:

* Gaul (modern-day France and Belgium)
* Britain
* Germania (part of modern-day Germany and Netherlands)
* Dacia (Romanian province)
* Illyricum (parts of modern-day Croatia, Albania, Bosnia and Herzegovina)
* Sicily
* Italy (including the Italian Peninsula, Sardinia, Corsica, and parts of Switzerland and Austria)

In Africa:

* Cyrenaica (part of modern-day Libya)
* Egypt (Romanized under Cleopatra VII in 30 BCE)
* Mauretania (parts of modern-day Morocco and Algeria)

And in Asia:

* The Middle East: Galatia, Cilicia, Lycaonia, Pontus (regions spanning parts of Turkey), Armenia Minor
* India: Indus region, bordering modern-day Pakistan
* Arabia Petraea: Southern parts of modern-day Jordan, Israel, the West Bank, and the Sinai Peninsula

Roman territories were often ruled indirectly through client kingdoms, such as Numidia in Africa and Armenia, or through treaties with other regional powers.

Approximately 200 million people lived within this vast territorial complex, a sizeable percentage of the world's population at that time.
