Summarise the events of the book 
 
The book "Frankenstein" by Mary Shelley is a classic novel that tells the story of Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who creates a monster from dead body parts. The story begins with Robert Walton, an explorer who is on a mission to reach the North Pole. He and his crew rescue Victor's creature, which has escaped from its creator, but ultimately rejects it.

Victor then recounts his own story of how he created the creature using alchemy and electricity, but was horrified by its grotesque appearance. The creature comes to life and begins to learn at an incredible rate, but is shunned by its creator due to its deformity.

The creature seeks revenge against Victor for abandoning it, leading to a series of tragic events that ultimately result in the deaths of several people close to Victor, including his younger brother William, his best friend Henry Clerval, and his fiancée Elizabeth.

The story takes a dark turn as the creature becomes more aggressive and violent, culminating in a confrontation between the two at the Arctic Circle. Victor's father, Alphonse Frankenstein, is also killed by the creature, leading to a sense of guilt and responsibility from Victor.

Throughout the novel, themes of isolation, loneliness, and the consequences of playing God are explored. The book ultimately ends with the death of Victor, who has become increasingly consumed by his own guilt and sorrow over creating the monster.

Key events include:

* Creation of the creature
* Rejection by its creator due to deformity
* Revenge from the creature against Victor's loved ones
* Death of several people close to Victor, including William, Clerval, Elizabeth, and Alphonse Frankenstein
* Final confrontation between Victor and the creature at the Arctic Circle
* Death of Victor

What are the major themes of the book? 
 
The major themes of Frankenstein by Mary Shelley include:

1. **The Dangers of Unchecked Ambition and Pride**: Victor Frankenstein's obsessive desire to create life leads to his downfall and the tragic consequences that follow.
2. **The Nature of Monstrosity**: The novel explores what makes a creature monstrous, not just physically but also morally and socially. This theme is reflected in the character of the Creature, who is often misunderstood and mistreated by society.
3. **Loneliness and Isolation**: Many characters in the novel, including Frankenstein himself, experience profound loneliness and isolation due to their circumstances or the consequences of their actions.
4. **The Consequences of Playing God**: The novel raises questions about the ethics of scientific inquiry and the responsibility that comes with creating life.
5. **Love, Friendship, and Human Connection**: The relationships between characters like Frankenstein and his friend Henry Clerval, as well as the Creature's longing for companionship, highlight the importance of human connection in our lives.
6. **The Social and Class commentary**: The novel touches on themes related to class, social status, and the treatment of those considered "other," such as the creature and Justine Moritz.

These themes are intricately woven throughout the narrative, inviting readers to reflect on the complexities of human nature and society.
